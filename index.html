the question

Can you prove a privileged action was authorised before it executed — not just logged afterwards?

Logs written after the fact show what was recorded. On their own, they don't prove an action was authorised before it ran. SIRDI is designed to record the decision at the point of authorisation, before the action runs, in a signed, tamper-evident receipt that can be checked with a standalone verifier.

the gap

Authorisation and evidence

where controls are missing
✕ Privileged AI-agent actions may run without a prior authorisation check
✕ Independent review is harder when the only evidence is a log controlled by the operator who ran the action
✕ An operator's account may need corroborating during an insurance claim, regulatory review or client dispute
✕ If approval isn't linked to the action, reviewers may have to reconstruct it from emails, tickets and logs
✕ Unmanaged AI actions may lack a traceable, tamper-evident record of prior authorisation
what SIRDI is designed to add
✓ Designed to check privileged actions before they run, deny-by-default; live enforcement depends on an integrated execution adapter
✓ Receipts can be checked with the standalone kit against a supplied public key, without the operator's runtime logs
✓ Hash-chained receipts provide tamper-evident evidence for recorded decisions
✓ Designed to sit between any agent framework and the systems it acts on; current evidence comes from scripted scenarios in a controlled benchmark harness
the mechanism

How SIRDI is designed to work

01
Intercept
SIRDI is designed to place a governance gate between an AI agent and the system it acts on, so privileged actions reach the gate before execution.
02
Evaluate
SIRDI is designed to run policy and any required human-approval checks before a decision is issued, and to bind each approval to the exact action and scope it covers.
03
Sign
A tamper-evident receipt is generated, signed with Ed25519 and hash-chained to previous decisions. Receipts from the SIRDI benchmark harness are signed this way and verify with the standalone kit.
04
Gate
An ALLOW or DENY decision is issued before the action proceeds. The benchmark harness demonstrates this for modelled actions; live enforcement needs an execution adapter validated in your environment. The receipt is portable: a third party can check it with the standalone kit, without access to your runtime.
the verifier

Six-layer verification

The first five checks run on the receipt bundle alone. Replay detection also needs the session registry.

L1
Structure Receipt schema validated against the frozen v0.1 specification. Malformed receipts are rejected immediately. CHECK
L2
Sequence Receipt counter and session binding verified. Out-of-order receipts flag chain integrity violations. CHECK
L3
Hash RFC 8785 JCS canonicalisation applied. SHA-256 payload hash recomputed and verified against the signed receipt. CHECK
L4
Signature Ed25519 signature verified against the governance public key. Tampered receipts produce a hard signature failure. CHECK
L5
Chain Each receipt hashes the previous receipt's hash. Chain continuity verified from genesis to current head. CHECK
L6
Replay Intent hashes are checked against the session registry, and duplicates are flagged. Unlike the first five checks, this needs access to that registry: a receipt bundle on its own cannot rule out a replay elsewhere. CHECK
